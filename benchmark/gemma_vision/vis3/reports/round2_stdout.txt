   A successful acknowledgement resolves the original race: the monitor samples sequentially, so a completed sample begun after clearing the target also establishes that the earlier sample finished. However, shutdown proceeds when acknowledgement times out.

   Concrete scenario: a native sample stalls for more than five seconds before reading the cold server’s `/proc` files. Detachment returns false, shutdown removes those files, and the delayed sample records an error. The warm gate or final sampling-error check can then reject otherwise successful work. This remains fail-closed for measurement validity, so it is nonblocking.

   Make acknowledgement failure stop the block explicitly after owned-server cleanup, and describe the bounded fallback accurately. A delayed-sample lifecycle test would cover this better than the current synthetic future-timestamp test.
